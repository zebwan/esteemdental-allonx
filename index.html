What is All-on-X

All-on-X replaces a full arch of missing teeth with a fixed set of teeth supported by 4 to 6 dental implants, instead of replacing every tooth one by one.

Your implants are positioned from a 3D CBCT scan and a digital plan, then placed with a computer-guided surgical guide. The final teeth are made in our in-house laboratory.

Guided surgery

Why Digital Guided Surgery

Planning the surgery and the final teeth together means fewer surprises, more accurate implant positions and a more comfortable procedure.

The planned implant position can be translated to the surgical site using a customised surgical guide, helping control the position, angulation and depth of implant placement.

Implants can be planned with the final restoration in mind, rather than placing implants based only on available bone. This helps coordinate implant position with the intended tooth position, bite and aesthetics.

3D imaging allows your dentist to assess the bone, teeth and surrounding anatomical structures before surgery, helping identify important considerations during treatment planning and avoiding unwanted surprises.

Because the treatment has been digitally planned beforehand, the surgical procedure follows a predefined plan, providing greater control during implant placement.

In suitable cases, guided implant surgery may allow implants to be placed through a more conservative surgical approach, potentially reducing surgical trauma and postoperative discomfort.

Digital planning allows the surgical and restorative stages to be considered together, helping create a more coordinated treatment workflow from planning → implant placement → final restoration.

FAQ

All-on-X Questions,
Answered

The things patients ask us most before starting full-arch treatment.

All-on-X is a full-arch implant treatment concept where a complete set of replacement teeth is supported by strategically placed dental implants.

Instead of replacing every missing tooth individually, several implants are carefully positioned to support a fixed full-arch restoration. The exact number and position of implants depend on your anatomy, bone condition, bite and treatment needs.

Suitability depends on factors such as your remaining teeth, bone volume, gum health, bite, general health, oral hygiene and treatment expectations.

A comprehensive clinical examination and 3D CBCT assessment are required before determining whether All-on-X is appropriate for you.

The number of implants depends on your individual anatomy and treatment plan.

While some full-arch concepts can be supported by four or six implants, other cases may benefit from additional implants. The decision is based on factors including available bone, implant positioning, bite, the design of the final restoration and long-term treatment considerations. There is no single number that is right for every patient.

In selected cases, a temporary fixed restoration may be attached to the implants on the day of surgery or shortly afterwards.

However, this is not suitable for every patient. Immediate loading depends on factors such as implant stability, bone quality, implant position, bite and the overall surgical plan. Your dentist will determine whether immediate or delayed loading is appropriate for your case.

Insufficient bone does not necessarily mean that All-on-X is impossible.

Depending on your anatomy, treatment may involve bone grafting, sinus augmentation, alternative implant positioning or other techniques to create a suitable foundation for the implants. Your CBCT scan allows us to assess the available bone and determine the most appropriate approach for your case.

Implants generally require a period of 3 months for healing and osseointegration, during which the implant becomes integrated with the surrounding bone.

The healing period varies between patients and depends on factors such as bone quality, implant stability, whether bone grafting was performed and the overall treatment plan. Your dentist will determine when the implants are ready for the definitive restoration.

Yes. The final restoration is designed to look and feel as natural as possible while also providing appropriate function.

The shape, size, colour and position of the teeth can be planned around your facial features, smile, bite and individual preferences. Our digital workflow and in-house laboratory allow the clinical and laboratory teams to work closely together when designing your restoration.

Good daily hygiene is essential for maintaining your implants and surrounding gums. You will generally need to clean underneath and around the full-arch restoration using tools such as:

  • A soft toothbrush
  • Interdental brushes
  • Super floss
  • Water flosser

Regular professional maintenance is also important to monitor the implants, gums and restoration.

The implants and the prosthetic teeth have different expected lifespans.

With good oral hygiene, regular professional maintenance and appropriate care, dental implants can provide long-term support. However, the prosthetic restoration may require maintenance, repair or replacement over time due to normal wear, fracture, changes in the mouth or other factors. Longevity depends on factors such as oral hygiene, smoking, bite forces, maintenance and the materials used.
